Carnival Cruise Line: is it right for you?

A Carnival cruise starts from about $105 per person per night for an inside cabin and from about $165 for a balcony in late 2026, so a week starts from roughly $735 for an inside cabin and $1,155 for a balcony, per person, before gratuities, drinks, and excursions. It is the best-value big-ship line for families, groups, and first-timers who want plenty to do and do not need a quiet, formal ship.

Carnival is the line most Americans picture when they think of a cruise: big, lively ships, a casual dress code, a water slide on deck, and a price that undercuts most of the competition. It is also the line people are most likely to be surprised by on the bill, because the low fare leaves out several things that most guests end up buying. Here is what you actually get, what it really costs, and who should book it.

What a week on Carnival actually feels like

Carnival calls its ships Fun Ships, and the name is honest. Expect a busy pool deck, music, trivia, comedy, and a nightly theater show, with a crowd that skews toward families, friend groups, and multi-generation trips. The mood is relaxed and the dress is shorts and flip-flops by day, with an optional dressier night or two. Food leans casual and generous, with a main dining room, a buffet, and free counters like Guy's Burger Joint, BlueIguana Cantina, and a pizzeria. If you picture a quiet, polished, adults-only sailing, this is not it. If you picture a good-value week where everyone in your group finds something to do, it is.

What it costs: starting prices

Cabin type drives the price more than anything else. These are late-2026 averages per person, drawn from a price tracker that follows Carnival sailings, and they are a guide rather than a quote, since a school-holiday week or a brand-new ship can cost well above them.

Shorter sailings are where Carnival is cheapest to try. In sample fares priced in 2025 and 2026, a 4-night Baja cruise from Long Beach started near $390 for an inside cabin, and a 5-night Western Caribbean sailing from Miami started near $525. Always compare the final price with taxes and port fees included, because that is the number you will pay.

What is included in the fare

Your cabin with daily steward service, meals in the main dining room and buffet, the free casual counters, and a handful of free drinks: water, iced tea, lemonade, milk, hot chocolate, select juices, and basic coffee. You also get the pools, the WaterWorks slides, the SportSquare activities, the fitness center, the nightly shows, and the kids clubs, which are Camp Ocean for ages 2 to 11, Circle C for 12 to 14, and Club O2 for 15 to 17. Visits to Carnival's own Bahamas destinations, such as Celebration Key and Half Moon Cay, cost nothing extra.

How to read a “from” price

A “from” fare is the starting price for an inside cabin. Your sailing can cost more depending on the ship, the week, and how close you book, so treat these numbers as a starting point and ask for a quote on your exact dates.

Where the extra money goes

This is the part to budget for before you book. The big four are gratuities, drinks, wifi, and excursions.

Bottomless Bubbles soda package$11.99 adult, $6.95 child, per day
Cheers alcohol packageabout $80 to $95 per adult, per day
Wifiabout $19 to $24 per day

Shore excursionsvaries by port

Gratuities are added automatically, so a week costs about $119 per person in a standard cabin. Drink packages carry a 20 percent service charge on top of the listed price, and if one adult in a cabin buys the Cheers package, Carnival requires every adult 21 and over in that cabin to buy it too. Prices move, so confirm each figure on Carnival's site when you book.

Do this before you compare fares

Add about $119 per person for gratuities on a 7-night sailing in a standard cabin, then compare ships. That one step makes cheap-looking fares much easier to judge.

What you will really spend

A useful rule of thumb is from about $150 per person per day for an inside cabin with light spending, and from about $200 per day for a typical guest who adds drinks, wifi, and a couple of excursions. A couple on a 7-night balcony cruise who drink at dinner and book two excursions should plan for well over the fare alone, and the extras can add 30 to 60 percent on top.

Is Carnival your kind of cruise?

Carnival is a strong pick for families with kids, because the clubs and water attractions are some of the best at sea. It suits groups and celebrations, first-timers on a short sailing, and anyone who wants the lowest entry price on a big ship. It is a weaker fit for travelers who want quiet, a formal atmosphere, or a ship that feels exclusive. For those, our Royal Caribbean vs Carnival vs Norwegian comparison shows where the other lines pull ahead.

Tips before you book

The ship matters as much as the cabin, since the newest ships have far bigger attractions than the oldest, and our Carnival ships guide explains the difference. Pick your sailing port first, because a drive-to port saves airfare, which we cover in the Carnival Caribbean itineraries guide. Decide on drinks before you sail, since the pre-cruise price is usually lower than the onboard price. And if this is your first cruise, read the first Caribbean cruise guide and the cruise packing list before you pack.

Our take

Carnival delivers the most ship for the least money, and it is hard to beat for a family or a group on a budget. Go in knowing the fare is the starting point, add gratuities and drinks to your math, and pick a newer ship if the attractions matter to you.

Common questions

How much does a Carnival cruise cost?

From about $105 per person per night for an inside cabin and from about $165 for a balcony in late 2026, or roughly $735 and $1,155 per person for a week, before gratuities, drinks, and excursions.

Are drinks included on Carnival?

Only basic ones such as water, iced tea, lemonade, and drip coffee. Soda, alcohol, specialty coffee, and bottled water cost extra, though you can buy a package. Bottomless Bubbles is the soda package and Cheers is the alcohol package.

Do I have to pay gratuities on Carnival?

Yes. They are added automatically at $17 per person per day in a standard cabin and $19 in a suite for sailings from April 2026, and they are paid on top of your fare.

Is Carnival good for first-time cruisers?

Yes. Short 4 and 5 night sailings are an easy, low-cost way to try cruising, and the casual atmosphere and included dining make it simple to learn.
