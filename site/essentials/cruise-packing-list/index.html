Cruise packing list: what first-timers forget

Pack a magnetic power strip, a small day bag, motion sickness remedies even if you rarely get seasick, and a lanyard for your ship card, since cabins have limited outlets and the first sea day catches most first-timers off guard.

Cruise ships pack a lot into a small cabin, which means a few specific items solve problems that only show up once you are on board. Here is what actually earns space in a first-timer's suitcase.

Cabin essentials cruise lines do not mention

Most cabins have very few outlets, so a magnetic power strip, not a surge-protected one which most lines ban, keeps phones, cameras, and CPAP machines charged without a nightly outlet standoff. A door magnet organizer holds daily schedules and small items on the metal cabin door, which is genuinely useful in a small space.

For getting on and off the ship

A small day bag or backpack matters more than it sounds, since you carry it through embarkation lines, port excursions, and back on board through security. A lanyard or card holder for your ship key card saves you patting down pockets at every door and the bar.

For the first sea day

Motion sickness remedies are worth packing even if you rarely feel seasick, since the first day out can catch anyone off guard in open water before the ship settles into its route. Ginger chews, wristbands, or over-the-counter medication all work, pick before you need it rather than searching the ship's small shop mid-crossing.

Clothing by day type

Pack layers for the ship's strong air conditioning, swimwear for pool and port days, and one or two slightly dressier outfits if your line has a formal or elegant night. Comfortable walking shoes matter more than cruise-specific gear, since port days often mean several hours on foot.

What people forget most

A printed copy of your booking confirmation and ID, in case a phone battery dies at exactly the wrong moment during embarkation. Cash in small bills for ports where cards are not always accepted. Reusable water bottle, since most ships let you fill it at stations around the ship, saving both money and trips to the bar for water.

Common questions

Do cruise cabins have enough outlets?

Rarely more than one or two, which is why a magnetic power strip (not a surge-protected one, which most lines prohibit) is one of the most useful items to pack.

Should I pack seasickness medication even if I don't usually get seasick?

Yes, the first sea day catches many first-timers off guard before the ship settles into its route, and it is easier to have it on hand than to search a small ship shop mid-crossing.

What should I bring for getting on and off the ship at ports?

A small day bag for essentials, your ship key card on a lanyard, and comfortable walking shoes, since port days often involve several hours on foot.
