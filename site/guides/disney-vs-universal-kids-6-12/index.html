Disney or Universal for kids ages 6 to 12

For ages 6 to 8, Disney World is usually the easier fit, and for ages 9 to 12 who like coasters, Universal Orlando is a serious contender, with one-day tickets starting from about $119 per person at both.

Quick take

  • Ages 6 to 8Disney is usually easier
  • Ages 9 to 12Either works, Universal for thrill fans
  • TicketsFrom about $119 per day
  • Days needed8 to 10 for both resorts

If your kids are somewhere between 6 and 12, you are in the sweet spot: old enough for real rides, young enough that the magic still works. The better resort depends less on the age number and more on what your kids actually like.

The short answer

Younger kids in this range, around 6 to 8, usually do best at Disney, which has a lot of rides without height limits and many gentle attractions. Kids who are 9 and up are ready for Universal, especially if they love coasters, Harry Potter, or Nintendo. If your group is mixed, Disney is the safer first trip and Universal is the one to add later.

Rides and height limits

Disney skews toward all ages and all comfort levels. Universal skews toward intensity, and many headline rides have minimum heights or intense motion. One example is Harry Potter and the Battle at the Ministry at Epic Universe, which has a 48-inch minimum. Always check each ride's current height rule before you promise anything, and remember that both parks offer a rider switch, so one adult can stay with a smaller child.

How to read these prices

Theme park tickets change by date. A Tuesday in mid-September costs far less than a Saturday in late December, so price your exact dates before you decide.

Child pricing generally covers ages 3 to 9 at both resorts, so a 10 to 12 year old is usually priced as an adult. Check the current ticket rules at checkout. Neither resort includes line-skipping in a standard ticket: Disney sells Lightning Lane separately, and Universal sells Express separately.

Crowds and line-skipping

Disney's Lightning Lane Multi Pass can help on busy days, especially at Magic Kingdom. Universal Express is worth considering at Epic Universe while crowds remain high. For both, weekdays in late August or early September are usually the easiest, and arriving early helps.

How many days do you need?

Disney has four parks and Universal has four, including Epic Universe and Volcano Bay. Doing both well takes about 8 to 10 days, with 4 to 5 at Disney and 2 to 3 at Universal, and Epic Universe needs at least one full day. If you have only a week, pick one resort and save the other for another year.

Skip these for this age

Universal's Halloween Horror Nights is not for kids under 12 or 13. If you visit in the fall, ask yourself honestly how your kids handle scary scenes.

So which one?

Choose Disney if your kids love characters, gentler rides, and shows. Choose Universal if they chase thrills and big worlds. Choose both if you can give the trip at least a week and a half.
