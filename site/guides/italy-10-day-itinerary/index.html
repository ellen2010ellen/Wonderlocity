A 10-day Italy itinerary for first-timers, with costs

The best 10-day Italy itinerary for first-timers is 4 nights in Rome, 3 in Florence, and 3 in Venice, connected by fast trains, and it costs $4,000 - $7,500 per couple in 2026 including round-trip US flights, mid-range hotels, trains, food, and the big sights.

Ten days is the sweet spot for a first Italy trip. It is long enough to do the three headline cities properly without the trip turning into a checklist sprint, and the fast trains between them mean you never lose a day to travel. Here is the route, the day-by-day plan, and what it actually costs.

The route at a glance

Fly into Rome, out of Venice, and move north in one direction: Rome for 4 nights, Florence for 3, Venice for 3. Open-jaw flights cost about the same as a round trip to one city and save you a half-day backtrack at the end. The Rome to Florence train takes about 90 minutes and Florence to Venice just over 2 hours, so every transfer is a morning, not a lost day.

Days 1 to 4, Rome

Give Rome your longest stay because it has the most ground to cover and you will land jet-lagged. Day 1 is arrival, a walk through the centro storico, and an early night. Day 2 is the ancient core: Colosseum, Forum, and Palatine Hill on one timed ticket booked weeks ahead. Day 3 is the Vatican, with an early-entry museum slot to beat the crush at the Sistine Chapel, then St. Peter's. Day 4 is the joy of Rome without a schedule: Trastevere, the Pantheon, the fountains, and the best carbonara of your life.

Days 5 to 7, Florence and Tuscany

Take a morning train on day 5 and be at your Florence hotel by lunch. That afternoon is the Duomo complex and a climb of either the dome or the bell tower. Day 6 is art day: the Uffizi in the morning and the Accademia for David in the late afternoon, both on timed reservations. Day 7 is a Tuscany day trip, either a small-group Chianti wine tour or the bus to Siena, which many first-timers end up calling their favorite day of the trip.

Days 8 to 10, Venice

Arrive day 8 by late morning and spend the afternoon getting purposely lost, since being lost in Venice is the attraction. Day 9 is St. Mark's Basilica and the Doge's Palace early, then the quiet Dorsoduro and Cannaregio neighborhoods once the day-trippers flood in. Day 10 is a morning vaporetto ride down the Grand Canal or a quick hop to Murano and Burano before an evening flight home, or a last spritz if you fly the next morning.

What it costs in 2026

These ranges cover two people for the full 10 days in shoulder season, with the low end for careful mid-range travel and the high end for four-star comfort in peak months.

Round-trip US flights, open-jaw$1,300 - $2,400 per couple
Hotels, 10 nights mid-range$1,500 - $3,000 per couple
Trains between cities$100 - $200 per couple
Food, sights, and local transit$1,100 - $1,900 per couple

Add roughly 20 to 30 percent for June through August travel, and subtract about 15 percent for November through March city trips.

Getting between cities

The Frecciarossa and Italo high-speed trains are the whole reason this route works. Book 2 to 4 weeks ahead on either operator's site and a Rome to Florence seat costs $25 - $50 per person instead of the $80 walk-up fare. There is no security line, stations are in the city centers, and luggage just goes on the rack, so a transfer door to door is usually under three hours.

When to go and when to book

April, May, September, and October are the sweet spots, with warm weather and prices below the summer peak. Book flights 3 to 5 months out, hotels 2 to 4 months out, and the timed tickets for the Colosseum, Vatican, and Uffizi as soon as your dates are fixed, since the best entry slots sell out weeks ahead in high season. Common questions

Is 10 days enough for Italy?

Yes, for a first trip. Ten days covers Rome, Florence, and Venice properly with a Tuscany day trip included. Trying to add the Amalfi Coast or Cinque Terre in the same 10 days usually means enjoying all of it less.

How much does 10 days in Italy cost for two people?

$4,000 - $7,500 per couple in 2026, including round-trip US flights, mid-range hotels, trains, food, and major sights. Summer peak adds 20 to 30 percent, and winter city trips run about 15 percent less.

Do I need to rent a car in Italy?

Not for this itinerary. High-speed trains connect Rome, Florence, and Venice faster than driving, and cars are a liability in all three cities. Rent one only if you add a countryside stay in Tuscany.
