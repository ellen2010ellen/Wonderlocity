Things to do with kids in Virginia Beach

Virginia Beach works for kids of every age because the beach is only the start: there is a big aquarium, a state park with 19 miles of trails, a 30-attraction water park, treetop courses, and indoor options for rainy days.

Quick take

  • Best forAges 3 to 12, and teens too
  • Beach and boardwalkFree to enjoy
  • Rainy dayAquarium, Apex, aviation museum
  • Plan on2 to 4 days

Most families come to Virginia Beach for the sand and then discover how much else fits around it. You can swim before lunch, watch sharks after, and be on a forest trail by late afternoon, all without a long drive. Here is how to mix it up so nobody gets bored, and what to save for the days the weather does not cooperate.

Start with the beach and boardwalk

The beach and the Boardwalk cost nothing to enjoy, which makes them the easy anchor for every day. The Boardwalk is good for biking and strolling, so it is an easy evening walk after a beach day. For little ones, Grommet Island Park is an ocean-themed playground steps from the sand, designed for children of all abilities.

See sharks and rays at the Virginia Aquarium

The Virginia Aquarium and Marine Science Center is the classic move for any weather. It has more than a dozen species of shark, ray, eel and reptile, plus live animal encounters, and it suits kids of all ages. Go early on a hot or rainy day, and check current tickets and hours before you head over.

Walk it off at First Landing State Park

First Landing is called Virginia's most visited state park, with 19 miles of all-ages terrain and 1.25 miles of Chesapeake Bay coastline. It is the right answer when the kids have had enough screens and sand, and you want a flat walk that nobody complains about.

Pack for the park

Bring bug spray, water, and a snack. A short, easy trail that ends with a beach stop beats a long hike for most kids.

Splash at Ocean Breeze Waterpark

Ocean Breeze has more than 30 attractions, including water slides, aquatic playgrounds, a wave pool, and a lazy river called Adventure River. It is a good day for mixed ages, because there is shallow play for the small ones and bigger slides for everyone else.

Rainy days and big-kid energy

When it pours, Apex Entertainment brings indoor ropes courses and go-karts. The Military Aviation Museum shows off about 70 planes restored to flying condition, which is a hit with plane fans of any age. The Cape Henry Lighthouse, built in 1792, has 191 narrow, winding steps, so decide if your kids are ready for the climb.

Farms, treetops, and wildlife

Hunt Club Farm is a family-run 50 acres with a petting farm and a TreeWalk Adventure. The Adventure Park at Virginia Beach is another treetop option for kids who like climbing. Back Bay National Wildlife Refuge is a quieter pick for wildlife viewing.

A simple three-day plan

Day 1Beach morning, Boardwalk walk in the evening
Day 2Virginia Aquarium, then Grommet Island Park
Day 3First Landing State Park or Ocean Breeze, depending on the weather

Keep one flexible day for rain or tired kids, and book the aquarium or water park for the day with the best forecast.

Where to stay

Staying near the oceanfront means you can walk to the beach and the Boardwalk and skip the car on most evenings. If you want a bigger space for the family, look for a suite or condo with a kitchenette, since breakfasts and snacks add up fast.

Common questions

What is the best thing to do with kids in Virginia Beach?

Start with the beach and Boardwalk, which are free to enjoy, and add the Virginia Aquarium and First Landing State Park. Together they cover water, animals, and an easy walk.

What can we do in Virginia Beach when it rains?

Try the Virginia Aquarium, Apex Entertainment for indoor ropes and go-karts, or the Military Aviation Museum, which has about 70 planes restored to flying condition.

Is Virginia Beach good for toddlers?

Yes. Grommet Island Park is an ocean-themed playground by the sand, and the aquarium and the beach both work well for small children.
