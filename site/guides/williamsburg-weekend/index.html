A Williamsburg weekend from Virginia Beach: what to do

A Williamsburg weekend from Virginia Beach costs $400 - $1,000 for two people in 2026, covering two nights in a hotel, gas for the roughly one-hour drive, meals out, and tickets to Colonial Williamsburg or a day at Busch Gardens.

Williamsburg is the closest real getaway Virginia Beach has. It is under an hour away, yet it swaps the boardwalk for brick sidewalks, fife and drum corps, and one of the best theme parks on the East Coast. It works as a history weekend, a roller coaster weekend, or an eat-and-wander weekend, and it is cheap enough that you can decide on Thursday and go on Saturday. Here is how to do it well.

Getting there from Virginia Beach

The drive is about 60 miles and an hour on a clear day: I-264 west to I-64 west, through the Hampton Roads Bridge-Tunnel, then straight up the Peninsula to the Colonial Williamsburg exits. The tunnel is the variable. On summer Fridays and holiday weekends an HRBT backup can add 30 to 60 minutes, so leave before 9 am or after 7 pm and you will glide through. No flights, no ferries, one tank of gas covers the whole trip both ways.

What a weekend costs in 2026

These ranges cover two people for two nights, with the low end for an off-season trip built around free strolling and one paid attraction, and the high end for a peak-season weekend with a nicer hotel and a full day at Busch Gardens.

Williamsburg hotels are a quiet bargain. The town has far more rooms than a normal town its size because of the tourism base, so outside graduation weekends and the December holidays, $75 - $120 a night buys a solid stay and $180 - $220 puts you in the historic-area inns.

Colonial Williamsburg without overpaying

Here is the part first-timers miss: walking the historic area is free. Duke of Gloucester Street, the gardens, the taverns from the outside, the costumed interpreters going about their day, all of it is open to anyone. The admission ticket, about $50 per adult for a single day in 2026, is what gets you inside the buildings: the Governor's Palace, the Capitol, the trade shops where smiths and printers actually work. If you have never been, one ticketed day is worth it, and the trades demonstrations are the highlight. If you mostly want the atmosphere, budget your money for a tavern dinner instead and stroll for free.

Busch Gardens, history, or both

Busch Gardens Williamsburg runs about $60 - $90 per person for single-day tickets bought online in 2026, and it is regularly voted the most beautiful theme park in the country. The coasters are legitimate, the food is better than theme park average, and the park sits ten minutes from the historic area. A weekend gives you room for one full Busch Gardens day and one historic-area day, which is the classic split. With toddlers or grandparents in tow, swap the park day for Jamestown and an afternoon at the free Yorktown beach.

Do not skip Jamestown and Yorktown

Williamsburg is one corner of the Historic Triangle, and the Colonial Parkway that links all three is the prettiest drive in the region. Historic Jamestowne, the actual 1607 site, and the Yorktown battlefield are both run by the National Park Service and cost far less than the big attractions, roughly $15 - $30 per adult depending on passes. Yorktown also has a small riverfront beach and a walkable main street that makes a perfect low-key Sunday morning before the drive home.

A two-day plan that works

Day one: leave Virginia Beach by 8 am, beat the tunnel, and be walking Duke of Gloucester Street by 9:30. Do the ticketed buildings in the morning while the tour groups are thin, lunch at a tavern or in Merchants Square, then spend the afternoon on the trades shops and the Palace gardens. Dinner in Merchants Square, and if it is summer, catch the evening fife and drum march. Day two: Busch Gardens from opening until mid-afternoon, or the Jamestown and Yorktown loop via the Colonial Parkway, then roll home against light Sunday traffic and be back for dinner.

When to go

April, May, September, and October are the sweet spots, with warm walking weather and full operating schedules. Summer works but is hot and holds the biggest Busch Gardens crowds. December is a special case: Grand Illumination and the holiday decorations make it the most atmospheric month, and Christmas Town at Busch Gardens turns the park into a lights festival, so it is worth braving the cold at least once. January and February are the true bargain months, with $75 hotel nights, though some attractions run shorter hours.

Common questions

How far is Williamsburg from Virginia Beach?

About 60 miles, roughly an hour up I-64 through the Hampton Roads Bridge-Tunnel. Summer Friday tunnel backups can add 30 to 60 minutes, so leave early or after dinner.

How much does a Williamsburg weekend cost?

$400 - $1,000 for two people for two nights in 2026, covering the hotel, gas, meals, and tickets. The swing comes from the hotel tier and whether you add a Busch Gardens day.

Is Colonial Williamsburg worth the ticket price?

For a first visit, yes. About $50 per adult opens the Governor's Palace, the Capitol, and the working trade shops. Walking the historic streets themselves is free, so repeat visitors can enjoy the town without a ticket.
